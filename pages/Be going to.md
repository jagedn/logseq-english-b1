- ## When to Use It
	- **Plans and Intentions**: For actions that have already **been decided** or planned before the moment of speaking. (e.g., *I am going to visit my family this weekend.*)
	- Predictions based on **evidence**: When you see clear signs that something is going to happen in the near future. (e.g., *Look at those clouds! It is going to rain.*)
	- Sudden Decisions: For decisions made **spontaneously** at the moment of speaking. (e.g., *I’m hungry. I am going to make a sandwich.*)
- ## Grammatical Structure
	- ### Affirmative
		- Structure: `Subject + to be (am / is / are) + going to + Verb (base form)`
		- *Note:* The form of to be must match the subject (*I am*, *He/She/It is*, *We/You/They are*). The phrase going to stays together.
	- ### Negative
		- Structure: `Subject + to be (am / is / are) + not + going to + Verb (base form)`
		- *Note:* Contractions are common: *isn't going to*, *aren't going to*, *am not going to*.
	- ### Interrogative
		- Structure: `To be (Am / Is / Are) + Subject + going to + Verb (base form) + ?`
- ## Examples
	- We are going to travel to Italy next year. (Plan)
	- She is going to start a new job tomorrow. (Intention)
	- It is going to snow this afternoon. (Prediction based on evidence)
	- Are you going to call him later? (Question)
